Research

Most of my work is about exoplanets, from finding them in photometric data to asking what the known population says about how planets form and change. I work with small-telescope photometry, archival data from Kepler, K2 and TESS, and a good deal of Python.

Exoplanet demographics

The radius valley, the shortage of planets between super-Earths and sub-Neptunes, is one of the clearest features in the exoplanet population. Its measured shape depends a lot on method, though. With Shaukat Goderya and A. Sohaib, I compare radius-valley slopes across F, G and K dwarfs and look at how detection-based and measurement-based approaches differ. (Submitted, 2026.)

A second study, with G. Masood and A. Hasnain, looks at condensation-temperature trends in the chemical abundances of FGK planet hosts. Some earlier work read these trends as a trace of planet formation. Once Galactic chemical evolution is accounted for, we find no evidence for that. (Submitted, 2026.)

Transit timing variations

Since 2024 I have collaborated with ÇOMÜ Ulupınar Observatory in Turkey (PI: Mehmet Yüksek). I am building a Python pipeline that combines MCMC fitting with emcee and N-body simulations, and using it on three short-period planets. New photometry and archival timings together give a long enough baseline to refine their ephemerides and to test for additional, non-transiting companions.

Along similar lines, I contributed the transit photometry analysis to a new study of WASP-10b, now in press.

Stellar activity and habitability

M dwarfs host many of the nearest small planets in their habitable zones, and they flare often. I am measuring flare activity of M dwarfs in the Pleiades from TESS photometry (in preparation).

This picks up a thread from my undergraduate project at BZU, a comparison of habitable zones around M-dwarf systems. It is also how I hope to move toward atmospheric characterisation: working out what stellar radiation does to a planet’s atmosphere, and whether the planet can stay habitable.

Exoplanets in open clusters

My M.S. thesis at IST, supervised by Shaukat Goderya (Tarleton State University) and Fazeel Mahmood Khan (NYU Abu Dhabi), was a 30.5-hour photometric search for short-period, Jupiter-size planets in NGC 884 and NGC 869 with a 0.8 m telescope. I reduced and analysed more than 200 light curves with ISIS, VaST and my own Python scripts.

No hot Jupiters turned up, but the survey found 25 variable stars, four of them new candidates. The work was supported by an IST Master’s Research Grant and published in the Open European Journal on Variable Stars in 2024.

Other work

Galactic H I 21 cm emission

An independent project in 2025 using the 1.5 m PICTOR radio telescope. I took drift-scan spectra and wrote the calibration, RFI mitigation and line-fitting code myself. The derived column densities agree with the LAB survey to within about 15%. The results are archived on Zenodo.

Space situational awareness pipeline

During a research internship at the Sharjah Space & Astronomy Hub, University of Sharjah (May–July 2026), I built a modular Python pipeline for optical observations of satellites. It handles calibration and quality checks, Gaia DR3 astrometry, timing corrections, object detection, and identification of known objects from TLE/SGP4 orbits.

Materials for space habitats

With the Deep Space Initiative (2025–present), I run thermo-mechanical simulations in COMSOL and ANSYS to see how micrometeoroid impacts and thermal cycling affect window materials for space habitats.

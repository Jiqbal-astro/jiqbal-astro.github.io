About me

I am an early-career astronomer working on exoplanets, with research experience spanning planet detection, atmospheric characterisation, and statistical studies of planetary populations. I use transit photometry from ground- and space-based telescopes to detect planets and to investigate transit timing variations in known planetary systems, and I examine how observational biases and measurement uncertainties shape our understanding of exoplanet populations.

I completed my M.S. in Astronomy and Astrophysics at the Institute of Space Technology in Islamabad. My thesis, a joint project with Tarleton State University in the United States, was a photometric search for hot Jupiters in the open clusters NGC 884 and NGC 869. I am collaborator with Tarleton State University, United State and with ÇOMÜ Ulupınar Observatory in Turkey.

The questions I want to spend the coming years on are about what these planets are like, not only whether they are there. I am interested in their atmospheres, in how their host stars shape them, and in which of them could be habitable. At the moment I am studying flares on M dwarfs with TESS data, since that is where stellar activity and habitability meet most directly. In the long run, I want to understand what conditions make a planet suitable for life.

Outside astronomy, I write poetry, go hiking whenever I can, and think a great deal about philosophy, both the philosophy of science and the larger question of how to live well. I am also the author of The Pressure Generation: Why Today’s Youth Are Exhausted, Anxious, and Still Expected to Win, a book about the expectations placed on young people today and the toll they take.

Current work

The radius valley across FGK dwarfs

How the gap between super-Earths and sub-Neptunes changes with stellar type, and why detecting the valley is not the same as measuring it. Submitted, 2026.

Chemistry of planet-host stars

Condensation-temperature trends in FGK exoplanet hosts. We find no sign of a planetary fingerprint beyond what Galactic chemical evolution already explains. Submitted, 2026.

Flares on M dwarfs in the Pleiades

Measuring flare activity from TESS photometry, with an eye on what it means for planets around these stars. In preparation.

Transit timing variations

Refining the ephemerides of three short-period planets and looking for unseen companions, with ÇOMÜ Ulupınar Observatory.
